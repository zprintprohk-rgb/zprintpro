Common Sticker Materials Compared

Waterproof Stickers (PVC / Synthetic Paper)

PVC stickers use plastic film substrates with complete waterproofing, oil resistance, and tear durability. At 80-100 microns thick, they perform reliably in refrigerated environments without curling. Synthetic paper combines paper-like printability with plastic-like water resistance. Its key advantage is recyclability and lower carbon footprint. For food delivery and beverage labels requiring complete waterproofing, PVC is the clear choice. For cosmetics and organic food packaging emphasizing eco-image, synthetic paper better communicates brand values.

Clear Stickers (PET / BOPP)

Clear PET stickers create a "no-label" premium look. When applied to glass bottles or transparent packaging, consumers see only the design, as if printed directly on the product surface. This is ideal for serums, perfumes, and beverages. BOPP clear film offers higher stretchability and tear resistance, suitable for curved surfaces and outdoor long-term use.

Foil Stickers and Special Finishes

Foil stamping creates metallic highlights perfect for festive gift seals or VIP member badges. Spot UV coating adds dimensional gloss to logos. Foil areas typically stay under 15% of sticker surface to accent rather than overwhelm.

Standard Sizes vs Custom Die-cutting

Common standard sizes include 50×50mm, 60×40mm, 80×50mm, and 100×100mm. Circular stickers at 30mm, 40mm, and 50mm diameters are most popular. Custom die-cutting achieves any shape from brand logos to product silhouettes.

Bleed and Safe Zone

Reserve 3mm bleed to prevent white edges after die cutting. Keep all important text and graphics at least 2mm inside the trim line to avoid cutting errors.

Design File Preparation

The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) recommends bitmap resolution of at least 300dpi for digital printing. RGB to CMYK conversion typically produces color deviation ΔE of 8-12, with direct RGB printing potentially causing up to 30% color shift.

— ISO 12647-2 Digital Printing Standard

Color Modes: CMYK and Pantone Matching

RGB color gamut is wider than CMYK. Converting RGB directly to print causes color darkening and reduced vibrancy. For brands requiring precise color consistency across batches, Pantone spot colors are recommended.

Resolution and Vector Graphics

Bitmap images should be 300dpi or higher. Logos and text should use vector formats (AI/EPS/PDF) for infinite scalability. Convert all text to outlines before exporting to prevent font substitution errors.
